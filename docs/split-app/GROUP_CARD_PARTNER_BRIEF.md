# Provider qualification brief: Australian group-funded event card

Prepared 19 October 2026. Draft for founder review; not sent to any provider.

## Proposed programme

WhereYouAt is exploring an Australian consumer product for private groups organising activities. An organiser creates an event and collection link. Participants contribute fixed or explicitly agreed shares. Funds remain within a provider-approved arrangement until spending or return. One verified organiser receives authority to purchase the agreed activity through a virtual card, ideally provisioned to Apple Wallet. The venue uses its existing card checkout and need not integrate with us.

Example: eight adults contribute $40 each to a $320 karting pot. Once confirmed funds are available, a nominated cardholder books the activity. Unspent money is returned under agreed rules. Merchant refunds must remain traceable to contributors even after the event has closed.

Our intended distinction is that authorising a spender should not silently extinguish each contributor's defined rights. Please identify whether you can support this, or whether your standard terms would instead treat the funds as the organiser's personal property.

## Proposed pilot boundaries — subject to approval

- Australian adult residents; AUD; domestic fixed-price leisure activities.
- One nominated verified spender per event; no shared card credentials.
- Bank-funded contributions initially, using an approved PayTo, NPP or virtual-account route.
- Short-lived event pots, with agreed funding deadlines and closure procedures.
- No credit, overdraft, cash withdrawal, international remittance or unrestricted peer-to-peer cashout.
- Low balance and transaction limits to be set with the provider.
- Explicit consent to spending authority, fees, cancellation and refund allocation.
- wya supplies coordination, records and support; regulated responsibilities to be allocated in writing.

## Questions requiring written answers

### Eligibility and ownership

1. Do you approve this exact multi-contributor consumer use case in Australia? Is it treated as P2P, stored value, card funding, payments distribution or another category?
2. Who is the product issuer, account holder, cardholder and legal/beneficial owner at each point? Who owes each contributor repayment?
3. Can participants retain defined entitlements while delegating limited spending authority? Please provide the applicable contractual structure, not only confirmation that subwallets exist.
4. Are third-party contributions permitted? Must all contributors be onboarded and verified? Can non-app participants use a secure web flow?
5. What appointment, licence, registration or exemption must wya itself hold? Which functions do you perform and which remain ours?
6. How are customer funds protected on insolvency of wya, the issuer or a collection partner? What protections explicitly do not apply?

### Funding, cards and refunds

7. Which collection methods are approved, when are funds spendable, and what returns/dispute risks remain afterwards?
8. Can your programme provide both collection and card issuing? If separate partners are needed, specify settlement movement, liquidity requirements and account ownership.
9. Are personalised consumer virtual cards and Apple Wallet supported for this programme? Which manual, web or in-app provisioning routes are available, with which contracts and approval timelines?
10. Which spending controls are enforceable: amount, aggregate balance, time, geography, merchant identifier/category and transaction count? What can bypass online authorisation controls?
11. How are stand-in authorisations, offline transactions, overcaptures, delayed clearing and duplicate events handled? Who funds any resulting negative balance?
12. Can we freeze new spend and safely calculate refundable funds while authorisations are pending?
13. How are unused balances, partial refunds, late merchant credits and closed-card refunds returned to the original contributors? What identity/source verification is required?
14. Who manages disputes and merchant chargebacks, and who bears losses or guarantees?

### Commercial and operational terms

15. Provide all setup and recurring costs: minimum commitments, KYC, active/inactive wallets, cards, wallet tokens, contributions, spending, declined payments, refunds, disputes and support.
16. Specify reserves, collateral, settlement prefunding, minimum volumes and the cost of terminating or migrating the programme while refunds remain outstanding.
17. Is interchange sharing offered? Provide the exact net basis and exclusions; our base model assumes none.
18. What disclosures, customer terms, complaints procedures, reporting, audits and security certifications are required? Who owns each obligation?
19. What sandbox scenarios, production certification and reconciliation reports are available? What support is available during failed spending or refunds?
20. What milestones must be met before a limited real-money pilot, and what dates can be committed to only after approval?

## Requested response format

For each item, distinguish: supported under standard terms; supported through a custom programme; unsupported; or requires further assessment. Include the relevant product documents and an annotated money-flow diagram. Separate consumer-facing fees from wholesale programme costs. A generic statement that virtual cards or Apple Pay are supported is not sufficient to establish this programme's suitability.

## Founder decision rule

Proceed only when the actual ownership model matches the customer promise, all live-money responsibilities are assigned, full costs support a testable consumer price, and the provider supports the cancellation/refund lifecycle. If the programme instead requires organiser ownership, revisit the product promise explicitly rather than concealing the difference in the interface.
